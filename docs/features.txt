EZVAC ALPHA 3.0 FEATURES
================================

EZvac is a standalone HVAC and indoor-climate plugin for Paper 1.21.1 and its
forks, including Purpur. A climate system is the devices linked together in
one world; it has a hidden key and no name of its own (3.0).


SYSTEM MODEL
------------

- A system (SystemId: world + key) is whatever is linked together with the
  link tool. Its key is never shown or typed: a system upgraded from 2.x keeps
  its old group label as its key; a new one is #n from meta.next-system in
  hvac.yml, a counter whose numbers are never reused. A system exists only
  through its devices; there is no systems section.
- One sign thermostat is the controller (head) of a system, at most one per
  system. Thermostat labels may repeat (3.0); commands act on the sign looked
  at, never on a label. A system without a thermostat is headless: its devices
  stay linked together and idle until a thermostat is linked to any of them,
  which merges the whole headless system into that thermostat's.
- Every equipment unit is always in some system: created on its own, or
  unlinked, it is a headless system of one. Settings panels and RPM monitors
  are created unlinked (no system) and linked afterwards.
- A pool thermostat is the same sign device with the water target range. Water
  that would make a pleasant room is cold to swim in and a hot tub would be
  unliveable as a room, so the two ranges overlap without either containing the
  other: rooms clamp to 60-85 F and pools to 75-104 F, both configurable.
  Since 3.0 a thermostat follows what is linked to it (HvacRegistry.settleHead,
  SystemLinks.pool): water-loop equipment or a loaded waxed copper grate makes
  it a pool thermostat, air equipment or an iron trapdoor a room one; an empty
  system decides nothing. A target still at the old range's default (72 or
  82 F) moves to the new default; any other is clamped. A system never mixes
  air and water (SystemLinks.conflict), which is what stops a pool range being
  used to heat a room to 104 F.
- A system may have one optional settings-panel sign. Without one, its
  operating profile is always NORMAL and matches the stock variable-speed
  behavior. When two systems merge and both have one, the absorbed system's
  panel is left unlinked.
- A system may contain any number of heat pumps, air conditioners, or furnaces.
- Equipment saves a tier (1-3; every unit is 2 in 3.0, and nothing reads it
  yet), so equipment tiers need no new save format.
- Heat pumps and pool heat pumps cool and heat; air conditioners cool only;
  furnaces and boilers heat only.
- Dispensers are authoritative actuators. Registered equipment cancels vanilla
  dispense behavior so inventory contents and redstone cannot fight the plugin.
- Equipment does not consume buckets, fuel, water, power, or other resources.
- Heat pumps, ACs, boilers, and pool heat pumps use water as their visible
  output. Furnaces use lava.
- Only the exact output source created and recorded by EZvac is retracted.
  Player fluid is never adopted merely because it is in front of a dispenser.
- Shared output blocks have one deterministic winner. Losing units stop and
  release their output before the winner starts.
- Rotated dispensers clean their previous owned output before claiming the new
  facing block. Unloaded cleanup is persisted and retried later.


CONTROLLER ORDER AND SAFETY
---------------------------

Every controller pass has explicit phases:

1. Validate equipment that physically delivered during the elapsed interval.
2. Advance compressor/blower RPM and calculate real delivered capacity.
3. Integrate each thermostat's room temperature.
4. Apply hysteresis, minimum-run, and minimum-off decisions.
5. Stop invalid, incompatible, disabled, blocked, or losing actuators.
6. Clean rotated/shared outputs.
7. Start the deterministic actuator winners for the new demand.

A failure in one thermostat or equipment unit is logged and isolated. It does
not abort every HVAC system for that controller pass.

Default thermostat behavior:

- Cooling starts above target + 1.0 F.
- Heating starts below target - 1.0 F.
- Cooling stops at target - 0.3 F.
- Heating stops at target + 0.3 F.
- Minimum run time is 300 ticks (normally 15 seconds).
- Minimum off time is 200 ticks (normally 10 seconds).
- Player targets are clamped to 60-85 F.
- Control stop boundaries are not clamped to the player target range. A 60 F
  cooling setpoint can correctly stop at 59.7 F, and 85 F heating at 85.3 F.

If a thermostat sign is unloaded, removed, disabled, or has no compatible
loaded equipment, demand fails safe to idle. The cached biome profile and world
clock still allow its simulated room to drift without force-loading chunks.


THERMAL AND MOTOR PERFORMANCE
-----------------------------

- One full-capacity unit cools 3.75 F per Minecraft hour.
- One full-capacity unit heats 5.0 F per Minecraft hour.
- Idle rooms drift 1.25 F per Minecraft hour toward outdoor temperature.
- Active outdoor load is 0.50 F per Minecraft hour.
- One Minecraft climate hour is 1,000 server ticks.
- Multiple units use square-root diminishing returns, capped at 2x capacity.
- Thermal integration is partition-independent across outdoor and control
  boundaries. Splitting the same ticks into multiple calls gives the same result.
- Heat-pump and AC capacity varies from 40% to 100%. Outside the default 3 F
  approach band they request full output; inside it they progressively slow.
- Every running heat pump and AC in a system receives the same variable-speed
  command. Their RPM-derived capacities are summed, so combined cooling slows
  in sync as the system approaches its target.
- ECO caps variable compressors at 2,400 RPM, NORMAL at 3,600 RPM, and TURBO at
  4,200 RPM by default. All caps are configurable. NORMAL is byte-for-byte the
  same RPM curve used before operating profiles were introduced.
- Variable compressors ramp from a 1,200 RPM minimum. The thermal simulator
  uses the capacity derived from actual ramped RPM, not merely the requested
  capacity or a cosmetic RPM display value.
- Furnaces and boilers are fixed-speed (EquipmentType.fixedSpeed): their
  blowers or pumps ramp to 1,100 RPM and their heat stays at full capacity.
  Before 3.1 boilers ran on the variable-speed curve by mistake.
- Fixed and variable capacity are accounted for independently. A slowed heat
  pump never averages a furnace below 100%; both contributions are added
  before the overall multi-unit diminishing-return cap.
- Profiles never affect furnaces or boilers. Variable units share one
  system-level profile command, including mixed AC/heat-pump cooling systems.
- Equipment tiers (3.1; model.Tier, saved as 1-3) are real equipment kinds.
  PerformanceModel.stagedRequest asks each unit for what its tier can do:
  single-stage 1 always; two-stage 1, or performance.tiers.two-stage.low-stage
  (0.65) within the approach band; variable the requested-capacity curve
  above, bit for bit. Compressors turn that into RPM on the usual curve;
  furnaces and boilers burn at that share (at least minimum-capacity), their
  blower at the same share of 1,100 RPM. Single-stage and two-stage units
  deliver tiers.single-stage.capacity (0.70) and two-stage.capacity (0.85) of
  it. Format 8 (3.1) marks every unit from an older file variable (Tier.LEGACY),
  at full capacity. The ETA and the thermostat's load follow each unit's tier.
- Very low startup RPM cannot create phantom heating/cooling or oscillate at an
  exact outdoor-temperature equilibrium.

Controller, thermal, variable-speed, airflow, and audio tuning values are
validated settings in config.yml. Invalid settings stop the plugin safely
instead of starting with an internally inconsistent controller.


OUTDOOR CLIMATE
---------------

Outdoor temperature follows Minecraft time, the biome, the height and the
season (2.1; before it, seven name-matched classes from Snowy 25/5 to
Hot 100/78):

- Every vanilla biome has its own climate in BiomeClimate: year-round average
  noon and midnight at sea level, a seasonal swing, and rain, snow or none.
  Examples: desert 98/64 +-14 (none), plains 72/50 +-18, jungle 88/74 +-2,
  taiga 52/32 +-24, snowy plains 28/8 +-26 (snow), ocean 66/60 +-10, caves
  steady (deep dark 52), Nether 96-120 steady, End 30 steady.
- biomes: in config.yml overrides any of it per biome id; values left out
  keep the built-in ones, and a broken entry warns and is skipped.
- A data-pack biome (Biome.CUSTOM) is looked up by its key through Paper's
  UnsafeValues#getBiomeKey for overrides, else estimated from vanilla's
  biome temperature and downfall: the mean follows the temperature, the
  day/night swing is 10 + 25 x (1 - downfall) F, the seasonal swing follows
  the mean (26 F when cold down to 3 F in the tropics).
- A rain biome snows where vanilla does: above sea level + 17, where the
  height-adjusted biome temperature is below 0.15.
- Altitude (altitude:): lapse-f-per-block (0.1) for every block above from-y
  (64), applied at the exact height after blending.
- Underground (underground:): depth = min(sea level, surface height) - y,
  using the MOTION_BLOCKING_NO_LEAVES heightmap of a loaded chunk, in worlds
  with a sun. A smoothstep from start-depth (4) to full-depth (20) fades the
  day/night swing into the daily mean, and fades the seasonal swing and the
  rain/snow shares to nothing.
- Seasons (seasons:): shift = seasonal swing x strength x
  sin(2 pi t / year - pi / 4), added to noon and midnight before the weather
  effect; t runs from the first day of spring, so mid-summer is +1 and
  mid-winter -1. Natural water uses 0.8 x the shift, water-lag-days (5) later.
  Not applied to the cached profile, only when a temperature is read.
- The calendar (SeasonService, seasons.yml): per world with a sun, a day count
  that moves on when the world's day count (full time / 24000) does, by at
  most one day per look; going back is ignored, so vanilla /time set day
  never resets the year. Time of day is the world's. New calendars start on
  spring day 1. /hvac season set|next|pause|resume changes it.

Profiles are cached per thermostat, so an unloaded thermostat never causes a
chunk load merely to sample its biome. A thermostat saves outdoorSeasonalF
once it has sampled since 2.1; before that it estimates one from its
daily mean.

Biome transitions (biome-blend: in config.yml, 2.1):

- The climate at a spot is the weighted average of biome samples every 4
  blocks within radius-blocks (default 24) at the spot's height. Weights fall
  linearly from 1 at the spot to 0 at radius + 4, so the middle of a biome is
  exactly that biome. Noon, midnight and downfall average; rain and snow
  become shares of the surroundings.
- Samples in unloaded chunks are skipped and the rest renormalised; with none
  loaded, the block's own biome is used. Blending never loads a chunk.
- Each 4x4x4 cell's blend is cached per world and forgotten when a chunk
  within the radius loads or unloads. Readings interpolate bilinearly between
  the four nearest cell centres, so there are no 4-block steps.
- Weather effects mix by share: a half-rain spot gets half the rain cooling,
  swing narrowing, wind and wetness. The HUD weather word follows the dominant
  weather (none unless at least half the surroundings get weather). Weather
  sensors report the real sky at their block.
- Thermostats cache the blended climate; on a border they save
  outdoorRainShare and outdoorSnowShare, otherwise nothing new.

Units (units: in config.yml, 2.1): F or C for everything shown and typed.
Typed temperatures accept an F or C suffix that overrides it. Internally, and
in config.yml, temperatures are always Fahrenheit.

Weather (weather: in config.yml):

- Rain cools the daily mean by 6 F, thunder by 9 F, snow by 3 F, and full rain
  narrows the day/night swing by 60%. The effect eases in and out over two
  in-game hours, because vanilla weather switches instantly.
- Only biomes that get precipitation react. Deserts, savannas, badlands, the
  Nether and the End ignore weather; snowy biomes get snow.
- Natural water follows only 10% of the air's shift.
- Outdoor wind (1.5 / 3 / 5 m/s clear / rain / thunder) lowers "feels like".
  With the daily wind (3.2, below) the day's wind replaces the clear 1.5, and
  rain and thunder add their difference from it.


WIND (3.2)
----------

- WindModel (pure) draws each day's level from SplittableRandom seeded by the
  world seed and day: the season calendar's day while it runs (so /hvac
  season set redraws it at once and /time set never replays one), else
  fullTime / 24000 (seasons off or paused). Weights calm 40, breezy 35,
  windy 18, gale 7, with windy and gale scaled by the season's windiness
  (spring 1.3, summer 0.7, autumn 1.3, winter 1.0; 1 without seasons) and
  calm taking up the difference. The day's mean speed is a seeded point in
  its level's range: calm 0-2, breezy 3-6, windy 7-12, gale 14-20 m/s.
- Speed now = mean x (1 + gusts x noise), gusts 0.25, noise a seeded value
  per in-game hour cosine-eased to the next, in [-1, 1]. Over the first 2
  in-game hours of a day the mean smoothsteps from yesterday's. Never < 0.
- WindService caches today's and yesterday's day per world and the speed per
  tick, keyed by that day. Only NORMAL worlds have wind. /hvac wind set
  holds a level for the rest of that day, in memory only; auto clears it.
- Exposure at a spot = biome exposure (blended like downfall; built-in table,
  wind.biomes overrides; unknown 1.0) x altitude (1 + (y - sea level) / 120,
  at most 1.5), capped at 1.8, x (1 - underground factor). Thermostats save
  it as outdoorWind (missing: 1.0).
- Outdoor wind = (day's speed + rain and thunder extras) x exposure; it is
  Outdoor.windMs. A reading's wind = that x (1 - influence) x (sky light /
  15)^2, so walls and roofs shelter a spot. Feels like uses it.
- Leak factor = min(maximum-infiltration 2, 1 + 0.05 x outdoor wind at the
  thermostat or standalone space). It scales ThermalModel's idle drift and
  active leak, HumidityModel's infiltration and AirQualityModel's
  infiltration. Pools take none.
- OutdoorAir: coarse x (1 + min(4, dust 1.5 x (1 - downfall) x (1 - wetness)
  x (max(0, wind - 6) / 8)^2)); fine x max(0.6, 1 - 0.03 x wind); pollen x
  min(1.8, 1 + 0.06 x wind). Rain washing applies after.
- Levels read from a speed by the midpoints between ranges: 2.5, 6.5, 13.
- Sensor.Kind.WIND is a LIGHTNING_ROD. HUD {wind} {wind_level}; placeholders
  wind, wind_level, wind_speed, wind_ms, wind_kmh, wind_mph; speeds in km/h
  with units C, mph with F, unless wind.units says otherwise.
- API 1.5: Wind (speedMs, Level) on ClimateReading and OutdoorClimate,
  Wind.Day, EzvacApi.windForecast(world, days), InstrumentView.Kind
  WIND_SENSOR.


HUMIDITY AND FEELS LIKE
-----------------------

- Moisture is simulated as a dew point and shown as relative humidity. Heating
  a room lowers its RH without changing its moisture; cooling dries it.
- Outdoor RH comes from the biome's vanilla downfall: about 30% at the daily
  mean in a desert, 54% on plains, 84% in a jungle or swamp. The dew point is
  steady through the day, so RH rises at night. Rain and snow pull it toward
  97%.
- Each air system's room drifts toward the outdoor moisture (15% of the gap per
  in-game hour). Delivered cooling pulls it toward the coil's 45 F dew point.
- Water bordering a room's airflow evaporates into it, scaled by how much of the
  space is water surface and by how much warmer than the air the water is. A
  heated pool hall gets muggy.
- Feels like is Steadman's apparent temperature: temperature, humidity and
  wind. Indoors there is no wind; at the edge of a system's reach it blends in.
- The HUD's comfort word, bar and boss-bar colour follow feels like in air.
  Water is judged by its temperature alone.
- The dew point never exceeds the air temperature; the excess condenses.
- Microvariance (2.0): point readings get a smooth, deterministic
  offset from value noise over a 6-block lattice that drifts over 3 in-game
  hours. At most 0.5 F in conditioned air, 1.5 F outdoors (blended by
  influence), 0.3 F in pool water, 0.8 F in natural water, and 0.3 F of dew
  point, so RH mostly follows the temperature. Thermostats, equipment, ETAs,
  evaporation and every other part of the simulation use the unvaried values.


DEHUMIDIFIERS
-------------

- An iron block on a waxed copper grate, controlled by a sign on the iron block.
  It belongs to no system.
- Its own airflow zone (30 blocks by default, a vent's reach) decides what it dries: the room
  of an air system whose airflow surrounds it, or else the zone itself, which
  keeps its own moisture and is what the HUD reads there.
- It dries 3 F of dew point per in-game hour in a space up to 1,500 cells,
  proportionally less in larger ones, and starts 2% above its setpoint.
- The water it removes fills a tank. Full, the grate is waterlogged and the
  unit empties for 30 seconds, then carries on. The grate's water is never
  allowed to flow.
- Click: its control menu, setpoint +5 / -5 within the presets' lowest and
  highest, and power. Sneak-click: power. Both need ezvac.use.
- When the dew point of the space it works on is at or below
  dehumidifier.dry-dew-point-f (35 F), there is nothing for its coil to
  condense: it keeps running, removes nothing, and its sign shows
  "Air too cold" instead of "Running" (cleared 1 F above). /hvac info says why.
- Standalone units (outside a system's room) whose zones share air are one
  space: one dew point, stepped once per cycle with their combined strength;
  the water dehumidifiers remove is shared equally between their tanks. Its
  size and water are its largest zone's.
- Humidifiers are the counterpart: an iron block with a waxed cut copper slab
  on top and a sign on the iron block. Same zone rules and controls; they add
  3 F of dew point per in-game hour in a space up to 1,500 cells, ease off as
  the air nears saturation, start 2% below the setpoint, and are plumbed (no
  tank). While running the slab is waterlogged and bubbles; its water never
  flows.
- Remote sensors are buttons: stone (temperature), polished blackstone
  (humidity), mangrove (both), birch (weather: Clear, Rain, Thunder or Snow;
  it needs open sky, judged by the MOTION_BLOCKING height map like vanilla
  rain, and shows Covered otherwise). A sensor hub sign shows up to nine of them,
  three per page; a click turns the page and auto-flip turns it every 5
  seconds. Sensors read exactly as a thermometer at the same spot, never send
  redstone, and can appear on several hubs. Hub signs follow the same
  near-players refresh rule as every other sign.
- Hygrometers and thermo-hygrometers are point sensors like thermometers:
  humidity inside and outside, or temperature, humidity and feels-like.


AIR QUALITY (3.2)
-----------------

- Every air system's room (saved on its thermostat as roomFine/Coarse/Pollen)
  and every standalone unit's space (zoneFine/Coarse/Pollen on each unit)
  holds three values: fine particles (PM2.5, ug/m3), coarse particles (PM2.5
  to PM10, ug/m3; PM10 = fine + coarse) and pollen (grains/m3). A space not
  yet simulated starts at the outdoor air. Pools have no air.
- Each value follows dC/dt = infiltration (outdoor - C) + sources
  - (settling + system filter + purifiers) C, solved exactly per step (an
  exponential approach to equilibrium). Infiltration is
  humidity.infiltration-per-hour. Settling per hour: fine 0.1, coarse 0.3,
  pollen 0.5. A system's own filter (fine 0.15, coarse 0.5, pollen 0.8) works
  only in a cycle its equipment delivered.
- AQI is the US EPA index, the higher of the PM2.5 (2024 breakpoints) and
  PM10 scores, 0-500, applied to the current value rather than a 24-hour
  average. The dominant pollutant reads as Smoke (PM2.5) or Dust (PM10).
  Pollen is not in the AQI; its level is Low/Moderate/High/Very high at 20,
  80 and 200 grains/m3.
- Outdoor air: a built-in table per biome (air-quality.biomes overrides it)
  of dry-day fine, coarse and spring-peak pollen, blended across borders with
  the climate and saved on the thermostat's cached profile
  (outdoorFine/Coarse/Pollen). Pollen x season (linear between mid-seasons:
  spring 1.0, summer 0.6, autumn 0.25, winter 0; summer's 0.6 without
  seasons) x (1 - 0.85 wetness); particles x (1 - 0.6 wetness). Underground
  pollen fades with the ground factor.
- Sources: the airflow scan notes lit-able blocks in or around the air
  (campfires, fire, lava, furnaces, smokers, blast furnaces, candles; at most
  256 per space). Whether each burns is read every 200 ticks from loaded
  chunks. Emission is per block at air-quality.reference-cells (1,500), scaled
  by reference / max(cells, reference / 4). Torches and EZvac's own furnaces
  and boilers never count.
- Air purifier: an iron block on a tube coral block (the filter) with a sign
  on the iron block, /hvac create purifier. Same zone rules as a
  dehumidifier. No setpoint: a mode, Low, Medium, High (speeds 0.35, 0.65,
  1.0 of its rate) or Auto (new purifiers; saved as mode). It runs whenever
  it is on, has a usable filter and can read the air. Auto idles on Low,
  steps to Medium past AQI 50 or at Moderate pollen, to High past AQI 100
  or at High pollen, and steps down only 20 AQI under a step with pollen a
  tenth under its level. Strength on High: fine 2.5, coarse 2.5, pollen 3.0
  per hour, x min(1, reference / cells); purifiers in one space add up by
  speed.
- Filter (filterLife 1..0): wears by what the purifier captured (the exact
  integral of its removal), scaled to the reference size and shared by speed
  between the purifiers of a space, over air-quality.purifier.filter-capacity
  (2,400; pollen weighted 0.5), plus running hours x speed over
  maximum-life-days (60). At 0 it stops and its coral is set dead without
  physics. The block is read every
  cycle: fresh coral where a full filter was is a new filter; dead coral is
  spent; anything else is "No filter". A part-used filter keeps its level.
  BlockFadeEvent on a purifier's coral is cancelled. Breaking the coral never
  removes the purifier (partRequired = false); the sign or iron block does.
- Readings: spot readings blend room and outdoor air by influence like the
  dew point (or a standalone zone's); /hvac info and /hvac temperature show
  it, but thermostats don't, as real ones measure no air; air monitor signs
  (Thermometer reads AIR) and cherry-button sensors (Sensor kind AIR); HUD
  {aqi} {aqi_band} {pollen};
  placeholders aqi, aqi_colored, aqi_band, pm25, pm10, pollen, pollen_count;
  API 1.5 AirQuality. air-quality.enabled: false hides all of it.


VENTS AND AIRFLOW
-----------------

The link tool (3.0; a glinting stick with the link_tool tag, which also
answers to the 2.x duct and sensor tools' tags) selects a thermostat (its
position is stored on the item) and links equipment, vent blocks, settings
panels and RPM monitors to its system, or selects a sensor hub and links
sensors. Sneak-clicking unlinks. Linking a member of a headless system merges
the whole system; a member of a system with another thermostat is refused.
Only explicitly linked blocks become vents.

A system conditions either air or water, decided by the vent blocks it uses:

- Iron trapdoors are air vents. They condition the room around them, and the
  system is fed by heat pumps, air conditioners, or furnaces.
- Waxed copper grates are water vents. Placed in a body of water they condition
  that water, and the system is fed by boilers and/or pool heat pumps. A pool
  heat pump heats and cools like an air heat pump (variable speed, operating
  profiles), so a pool can be cooled as well as heated. Only the unweathered waxed
  grate counts: waxed copper never oxidises, so a registered vent cannot change
  material on its own, and every vent in a build keeps one appearance.
  Scraping the wax off with an axe changes the block and unregisters the vent,
  the same as breaking it.
- A system must be all air or all water. Linking a waxed copper grate to a
  system that already uses iron trapdoors is refused, and the reverse likewise.
- Sources belong to a loop as well, and the two must agree. Boilers and pool
  heat pumps are the water loop; heat pumps, air conditioners, and furnaces are
  the air loop. Registering water-loop equipment for a system that distributes
  through iron trapdoors is refused, as is linking an iron trapdoor to a system
  powered by water-loop equipment. Water-loop equipment therefore never affects
  room climate, and an air heat pump never conditions a body of water.
- Water keeps its own temperature. Water no pool system reaches - lakes, the
  sea, an unheated pool - reads the biome's natural water temperature: the
  biome's daily mean pulled toward mild values, with no day/night swing and
  never below 33 F. Readings at the edge of a pool blend toward that natural
  water, not toward the air, and an idle pool drifts toward it the way an idle
  room drifts toward the outdoor air.
- Everything else is identical between the two. Water bodies behave exactly
  like rooms: the same thermostat, the same hysteresis and minimum run and off
  timers, the same capacity from multiple units, the same distance falloff from
  each vent, and the same ETA. More equipment heats faster; more vents cover
  more of the body.
- The walk itself follows the medium: air systems travel through passable
  blocks and stop at liquids and solids, while water systems travel through
  water and stop at everything else.

- Each vent seeds six-directional airflow.
- Passable blocks carry air.
- Liquids and solid blocks stop air.
- Open doors/trapdoors pass air; closed ones stop it.
- Carpets (wool and moss) pass air: a carpeted floor is still part of the room.
- Core distance 0-15 receives full thermostat temperature.
- Distance 16-30 blends progressively back to outdoor temperature.
- Beyond distance 30 there is no influence.

Airflow is event-invalidated and rebuilt incrementally. It never force-loads a
chunk, inspects at most 4,000 nodes per server tick by default, and caps a system
at 200,000 cells. Chunk events invalidate only vent systems whose possible
airflow radius intersects that chunk, instead of every system in the world.

Vents are indexed on a coarse grid at least as wide as the airflow range, so a
block change looks only at vents in the few grid cells around it rather than at
every vent on the server. Natural changes between two full solid blocks, such as
buried grass decaying to dirt or concrete powder setting, cannot open or close
an airflow path and are ignored outright. Both matter after large WorldEdit or
FAWE fills, which can produce thousands of such changes per tick.

Rebuilds are debounced. A system waits 20 ticks after its last invalidation
before rebuilding, so a door being toggled repeatedly does not restart the walk
on every change. A 200-tick ceiling guarantees that a constantly changing system
still refreshes. A rebuild already in progress is allowed to finish rather than
being discarded, so a repeatedly invalidated system always has a usable map
instead of never completing one. The practical effect is that airflow reacts
about a second after a door moves rather than instantly.


DISPLAYS AND AUDIO
------------------

- Signs are rewritten only within about 64 blocks (4 chunks) of a player.
  Farther signs keep their last text and catch up within a second of someone
  coming near; nothing else depends on sign text.
- Thermostat signs use a compact real-thermostat layout showing EZvac/ID,
  current room temperature, COOL/HEAT/IDLE/OFF, setpoint, load (3.1: delivered
  capacity over what the system's enabled units could deliver flat out in its
  profile, both after the diminishing returns, so 0-100%; before 3.1 it was raw
  capacity and passed 100%), the
  running/total units of its system (3.0; the group name before), and an
  approximate ETA while actively heating or cooling.
- ETA is forward-simulated from the current equipment mix, actual RPM, future
  compressor modulation, outdoor load, and the same thermal rules as control.
- The target can be changed by command or from the control menu a right-click
  on the sign opens (2.1; ezvac.use): +5/+1/+0.1 and -5/-1/-0.1 in the
  server's units, rounded to a tenth in that unit and clamped to the room or
  pool range, plus a power lever. No EZvac sign can be edited; line-3 target
  editing was removed in 2.1.
- Sneak-right-clicking the sign switches the system off or on (ezvac.use).
- Thermometers are independent point sensors with no stored system. Each reads
  its own sign block exactly like /hvac temperature: influenced vent temperature
  where airflow reaches it, blended edge temperature, or outdoor temperature.
  They may be placed outdoors or where no HVAC system exists.
- RPM monitors (reworked in 3.0) show their system's equipment, one row per
  EquipmentType in enum order: the kind, the count when more than one, and the
  average RPM of the running units ("off" when none runs). Rows are fitted to
  the sign, shortening the kind first. Three rows to a page (model.Pager,
  shared with sensor hubs): a click turns the page, a sneak-click toggles
  auto-flip (every 100 ticks), which is saved; the page is not. A 2.x
  monitor's equipmentType is read and dropped.
- Settings-panel signs show their system and ECO/NORMAL/TURBO profile. A normal
  right-click advances the profile; sneak-right-click moves backward. Removing
  or unlinking the panel immediately restores the system to NORMAL.
- Display writes are fingerprinted so unchanged signs are not rewritten.
- Screens (displays.screens, default true, 2.1): a non-persistent text
  display entity in front of each device sign (ScreenService), placed by sign
  kind (wall, standing, hanging) and facing, scale 0.4 (0.34 on hanging signs),
  100 px lines, a dark translucent background, full brightness. Every screen is
  6 lines (blank ones pad it) with its title padded by spaces to the full
  width, because a text display sizes its panel to its text. Text is sent only
  when it changed. A refresh puts back a screen that its chunk unloading took
  away; removing a device (DisplayService.invalidate), a periodic sweep of
  screens without a device, and plugin disable take them down. The sign stays
  behind and is still written, for clicks and for clients without screens.
- Sign lines are measured in pixels with the vanilla font's widths (90 px on
  standing and wall signs, 60 px on hanging signs) and cut to fit, so the
  game never wraps one. Headers drop "EZvac | " when the label needs the room,
  and a reading that doesn't fit is left off whole rather than cut.
- Breaking a registered device tells the player what was removed and which
  system it left, so a stray pickaxe cannot quietly unregister a thermostat.
  Thermometers are silent: they belong to no system, so there is nothing to
  warn about losing. Removals with no player behind them - explosions, pistons,
  fire - stay silent for the same reason.
- Removing the last equipment from a system also warns that its thermostat will
  stay idle until equipment is added again. Nothing is deleted: the thermostat,
  its target, and every linked vent survive, so replacing the equipment
  restores the system exactly.

Startup and shutdown sounds remain equipment-specific. Running equipment emits
finite, spatial beacon ambience from each dispenser on a configurable cadence.
Every sound is a "sound volume pitch" line under audio.sounds (2.0);
[] silences an event. Running air systems' vents near a player play the
conduit's ambient sound (one vent per 6-block cluster, every
vent-airflow-refresh-ticks); pools and water vents stay silent.
EZvac never performs broad sound/category stops, so it cannot silence an
unrelated beacon, furnace, or another plugin's audio.


CLIMATE HUD AND PLACEHOLDERS
----------------------------

- /hvac hud shows, per player, the local temperature, how warm it feels, a
  cold-to-hot bar, a comfort label (cold, cool, comfortable, warm, hot), the
  humidity, the outdoor temperature and weather, and the conditioning system
  with its mode, target, and core/leakage zone.
- Styles: a scoreboard sidebar (default, score numbers hidden), the action bar,
  or a boss bar whose fill and colour follow the temperature.
- Off by default per player; the choice is stored in the player's own data,
  so hvac.yml is unchanged.
- The sidebar never replaces a scoreboard in use: it attaches only while the
  player is on the server's main scoreboard and nothing else is shown there,
  steps back if another plugin later takes over, and is not used at all when a
  packet-based scoreboard plugin such as TAB is installed (the action bar is
  used instead; see hud.sidebar.yield-to-plugins).
- With PlaceholderAPI installed, %ezvac_temp%, %ezvac_feels_like%,
  %ezvac_humidity%, %ezvac_aqi%, %ezvac_pollen%, %ezvac_weather%,
  %ezvac_bar%, %ezvac_system% (also
  %ezvac_group%: the label of the serving system's thermostat, or Headless)
  and the rest
  expose the same values to other plugins. Without it, EZvac logs one
  line saying what installing it would add.
- In water the HUD uses its own layout and scale (hud.water): Water, Air, Pool
  and Circulation lines, a 60-104 F bar, and pool comfort bands where 78-86 F is
  comfortable and above 95 F is a hot tub. It reads at the player's eyes when
  submerged and at the feet otherwise, so swimming at the surface and wading
  count as being in the water. %ezvac_medium% reports air or water.
- In air, a player's climate is read at head height, falling back to the feet.
  Standing on a carpet, slab, stairs, or snow puts the feet inside that block,
  and the reading used to show the outdoor temperature there.
- Values come from one snapshot per player, refreshed on the main thread every
  hud.update-ticks (default 10). Placeholder requests from any thread only read
  that snapshot, so frequently refreshing HUD plugins add no extra lookups.
- Temperature lookups (HUD, /hvac temperature, thermometers) check only the
  systems with vents near the spot, not every system in the world.


PERSISTENCE
-----------

All thermostats, equipment, pending cleanup, vents, thermometers, RPM monitors,
settings panels, dehumidifiers, humidifiers and air purifiers live in one versioned
plugins/EZvac/hvac.yml file (format 7 since 3.0: every device saves its
system's key, equipment its tier, and meta.next-system the next system number;
a 2.x file loads with each group name as its system's key and is kept as
hvac.yml.format6.bak before its first 3.0 save, and a 1.x file likewise as
hvac.yml.format5.bak). There is no conversion back: 2.x refuses a format 7
file and leaves it untouched.

config.yml is brought up to date on startup: settings added since it was
written are added with their comments, nothing existing is changed, and the
previous file is kept as config.yml.before-<version>.bak.

- Records use immutable world UUID + integer block coordinates.
- World unloads require no detach/rebind rewrite.
- Saves use a temporary file, backup, and atomic replacement when supported.
- The save snapshot is built on the main thread; the YAML dump and file
  replacement run off it, so a large registry does not stall the server tick.
  Shutdown saves are synchronous, and concurrent writers are serialised.
- If the main file is unreadable, the backup is restored.
- Invalid records are logged and preserved instead of overwritten.
- Loaded missing blocks are pruned; unloaded blocks are retained.


PUBLIC API
----------

- Since 2.1 the build is two modules: ezvac-api (the public API) and
  ezvac-plugin, whose jar shades the API's classes in unrelocated. JitPack
  publishes com.github.EZvac-Plugin.EZvac:ezvac-api:<tag>.
- EzvacApi (version 1.1, read-only apart from HUD sections) is registered
  with Paper's services manager while EZvac is enabled (EzvacApiImpl in
  integration.api). Readings reuse AirflowService.temperatureAt /
  temperatureForBody, so they are the
  HUD's and /hvac temperature's numbers, variance included; outdoorAt reuses
  OutdoorTemperatureService.climateAt and seasonShiftAt; thermostat views take
  their unit counts from HvacController.status. Every value is an immutable
  record (ApiViews maps internals to them); temperatures are Fahrenheit.
- Events, main thread, built only while their HandlerList has listeners
  (ApiEvents): PlayerClimateUpdateEvent from the HUD's update loop, which then
  reads every online player (ClimateHudService.ReadingObserver) and keeps the
  latest reading per player for latestFor (any thread); ThermostatModeChange
  from the controller's thermostat loop (mode before vs after);
  ThermostatTargetChange from the control menu and /hvac thermostat set;
  EquipmentStateChange from each cycle's started/stopped lists;
  SeasonChange from SeasonService (season before vs after any calendar change).
- Devices (API 1.2): equipment(), vents(), instruments(), each also by world,
  plus thermostats(world), climateUnits(world) and devices(world). Views are
  the sealed HvacDevice (ThermostatView, EquipmentView, VentView,
  ClimateUnitView, InstrumentView), built in ApiViews from registry objects.
  A vent's medium follows its system: a pool thermostat, or else water-loop
  equipment, means WATER; no block lookups. HvacRegistry.DeviceObserver
  (EzvacApiImpl) hears runtime add*/remove* calls and pruning (MISSING),
  never load*; /hvac remove, /hvac remove system and link-tool unlinks run inside
  removingByCommand (COMMAND); a relinked vent is removed (COMMAND) and
  added again. ApiEvents fires DeviceAdded/DeviceRemovedEvent only while
  listened to.
- HUD sections (API 1.1, EzvacApi.hud()): HudSections in integration.api is
  the registry (id = plugin name, lowercased with spaces as underscores, ":" +
  key; registering again replaces; a plugin's sections go with its
  PluginDisableEvent). ClimateHudService asks it for a lazy SectionFrame per
  shown player per update; each section is drawn at most once per frame and
  only where the layout asks. HudFormatter.lines expands sidebar lines that
  are just {addons} (every section not placed elsewhere, in registration
  order) or {section:<id>}; the server's own lines always fit and a section
  that would pass 15 lines is left out whole. One-line formats replace
  {section:<id>} from the template with the section's inline(). A section
  that throws is logged once while it keeps failing and is left out.
  hud.sections.hidden switches sections off; /hvac hud sections lists them.
  HudSettings.addAddonsLine adds {addons} to stock sidebars on upgrade.

PERMISSIONS
-----------

- ezvac.use (default true): help, temperature, list, info, season, and using
  signs: thermostat targets and power (control menu), settings panels,
  dehumidifiers and humidifiers, sensor hubs.
- ezvac.hud (default true): show the on-screen climate HUD with /hvac hud.
- ezvac.admin (default op): create, change, link, remove, sync, and stats.

See commands.md for the full command reference.
